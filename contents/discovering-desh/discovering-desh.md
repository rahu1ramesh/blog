I have heard this song from [Pareeksha (1967)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareeksha_(1967_film)) probably 100 times before, but this time I also did pay attention to the lyrics -

There was a sense of longing in these lines, which in the context of Premam made it one of my immediate favourites.

Recently, while listening to *Tere Paas Main* from [Main Vaapas Aaunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Vaapas_Aaunga), my favourite from what I think is one of A. R. Rahman’s finest albums of 2026, I was reminded of *Oru Pushpam*. Unlike *Oru Pushpam*, [Irshad Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irshad_Kamil)’s brilliant lyrics do not dwell on loss or longing, but are a quiet promise of always being there.

<iframe data-testid="embed-iframe" style="border-radius:12px" src="https://open.spotify.com/embed/track/50EDY0zGZteoBy3hORXSba?utm_source=generator&theme=0&si=d35b159ebced49fb" width="100%" height="152" frameBorder="0" allowfullscreen="" allow="autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; fullscreen; picture-in-picture" loading="lazy"></iframe>

On the surface, both songs come from stories of a star-crossed romance, but *Oru Pushpam* is about longing like Gatsby while *Tere Paas Main* is more of a promise of always being with one. Somehow I couldn’t shake the feeling that somewhere these two songs had something in common. By this point I had created a habit of playing this song on loop whenever I was doing mindless work. While on one such occasion, [Youtube suggested a version of Tere Paas Main](https://youtu.be/lYXdJnMzsEo?si=l3_V9TwCa4uwQuPe) sung by [Ayushmann Khurrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayushmann_Khurrana). He starts off with *Tere Paas Main* and transitions into *Hum Tere Pyar Mein*, another one of my favourites sung by the late [Lata Mangeshkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lata_Mangeshkar).

<iframe data-testid="embed-iframe" style="border-radius:12px" src="https://open.spotify.com/embed/track/2rHxwYzdn66nDMmDmmio6W?utm_source=generator&theme=0&si=409443cad92147dc" width="100%" height="152" frameBorder="0" allowfullscreen="" allow="autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; fullscreen; picture-in-picture" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Now this got me intrigued. How is it that all of my favourites sound so similar? Or rather, the more interesting question: why do I keep gravitating towards songs that sound like this? A quick google search revealed a hidden (at least to me) connection. These songs are all based on the famous Hindustani raag, [Desh](https://tanarang.com/raag-des/)[^1].

<iframe width="100%" height="240" src="https://youtube.com/embed/Ix-BoQQDYkw?si=PoZpKoWDdKvCivH8" title="Raag Desh" loading="lazy" allow="accelerometer; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Now this information got me excited. I started looking up what other famous Indian songs were based on Raag Desh, and was pleasantly surprised to see a lot of my favourites. From *Nee oru kaadhal sangeetham* by [Illayaraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaiyaraaja) to *Vande Mataram* originally composed by [Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankim_Chandra_Chatterjee), all had Raag Desh at its core. The famous aalap from [Roja (1992)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roja_(film)) is also based on the same raag.

I still don't know what makes me like these songs, and this is far from the only music I love. Correlation is not causation and I don't want to believe that Desh (which was the only binding factor between these songs) is what made me like them. Every song has various moving parts to it and just the raag alone does not define the song. I am not sure if I would like some other favourite song of mine if it was re-rendered in Desh. For now, I take comfort in finding out why *Tere Paas Main* and *Oru Pushpam*, songs decades apart, sound the same. But why I am drawn to songs in Raag Desh is still a mystery.

[^1]: There were contradictory accounts on how *Tere Paas Main* is based on two other raags - Yaman and Bihag. I am not an expert in Hindustani, but I like the Desh connection more, hence we'll go with that.
[^2]: I have categorized these songs as based on Raag Desh relying on accounts online.
