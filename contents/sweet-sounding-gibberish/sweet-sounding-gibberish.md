[Anirudh Ravichandran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anirudh_Ravichander)’s latest song from [Rajinikanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajinikanth)’s [Jailer 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jailer_2) dropped last night. Titled _Ala Bolelo_, it is a peppy song with some catchy beats. The song also features literal gibberish that I think is meant to be Malayalam. While the Tamil portions of the song have actual Tamil lyrics that carry some meaning, the part in Malayalam (or a parody of the language) makes no sense at all. Apart from some words like “mole”, “paadu”, “odo”, “aadu”, the rest of the lyrics attributed to [Arunraja Kamaraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunraja_Kamaraj) is a mockery of the language spoken by [roughly 40 million people](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/50-most-spoken-languages-in-the-world-1194779).

This is not the first time Anirudh has produced a song which butchers Malayalam. His much popular ‘_Manasilaayo_’ from _Vettaiyan (2024)_ also featured lyrics in Malayalam with random words thrown together. That song, though face-palm worthy, had some meaning and resemblance to the language, but _Ala Bolelo_ takes it up a notch and is a bunch of sounds that would pass for a decent stereotype of the language. This poorly affects the enjoyability of an otherwise decent song.

Tamil movies have had songs with Malayalam in them before. From the much adored “_Sundari Neeyum_” from [Michael Madana Kama Rajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Madana_Kama_Rajan) to the recent banger "_Bum Ba Diga_" from Anirudh’s own album for _Arun Matheswaran’s DC (2026)_, with lyrics from rapper [Vedan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedan_(rapper)>), Tamil cinema has often integrated the sister language into its songs. Up until recently I found the use of Malayalam in Tamil songs rather pleasant. As a Malayali I’m not sure how well received these additions have been in the Tamil-speaking state, but to me the respect for the language through these songs shows a sense of warmth and camaraderie.

Tamil lyricists have recently been dumbing down the language, perhaps to make songs more palatable for the audience. I am not against Anirudh or Arunraja using gibberish. What I object to is passing that gibberish off as another language, and taunting that language with easy stereotypes of its pronunciation. One can argue that the song and its creators mean no disrespect, but even granting that, fun should not come at the expense of someone else.

In my brief time working from Coimbatore, I have met people who find the language sweet and interesting, and the others who reduce it to [“Shamalamadingdong”](https://www.vice.com/en/article/what-a-twist-m-night-shyamalan-doesnt-deserve-to-be-a-running-joke/?/). I am okay with both, for they represent themselves here more than the language itself. But when an artist does that, it becomes the mainstream representation of the language. In the second season of the Amazon web series [Vadhandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadhandhi_season_2), the cop played by [Sasikumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Sasikumar) tries to talk to another person in Malayalam. When asked, he simply replies that he liked the language and learnt to speak and read it out of interest. Even if you don’t spark an interest in the language among your audience, you can at least try not to ridicule it. An artist in my view should elevate the taste of their audience, never degrade it for a cash grab.
