Some time back in late 2024, almost two years after I joined Bahmni, there was a point when the team was downsized from about ten to twelve developers, to just two. In the glorious days of Bahmni, it had about a hundred developers working on multiple streams, so dropping to just two was a severe blow. One person was chosen to stay, and they wanted to keep one more. I had already started packing my bags, gearing up for a teary goodbye, but the product manager asked me whether I would choose to stay, given the option. I was unsure, much like I'm right now, and I was asking for advice from anyone and everyone I knew.

The advice that won out was to leave the project and look for an opportunity on a client project, for this would make me a more valuable asset in the ThoughtWorks ecosystem. I expressed my concerns to the product manager, and she asked me to talk to our tech architect. Though I had had multiple brief check-ins up until that point, I hadn't worked with him enough, and I was unsure, a little intimidated. I went to him and laid out my case. We talked for about thirty minutes, and the one thing he said that stayed with me was this: "Every other experience you are looking for will find its way into your life at some point, but being part of an open source project is not something people get to experience in a lifetime, and this might be the only shot you'll get at it."

This convinced me, and I went back wanting to stay. But things had already changed, and a senior developer was in the race with me, also wanting to stay on with Bahmni. She was my mentor when I first joined Bahmni, far more technically capable than I ever was, and far more hardworking. Yet the tide was in my favour; something happened, and she had to move on to a different project. From then on, I've had this lingering feeling of living my life on borrowed time, time and opportunity that had belonged to someone else and been loaned to me.

## Nullum gratuitum prandium

In the last two years, I have had the time of my life working on Bahmni, trying to make the most of it. It was around then that [Open Healthcare Network (OHC)](https://ohc.network/) reached out to us, wanting to try out their AI-assisted clinical scribe extension, [Medispeak](https://medispeak.in/) (a tool that writes notes during a patient visit based on live clinician conversations), on Bahmni. Working on it made me curious about AI, a field I had not explored before. The team at OHC helped me understand their extensions, which they had built over a weekend.

This was soon followed by ThoughtWorks wanting to experiment with AI on an existing (brownfield) project, trying to figure out how best to use coding agents and lay the foundations for [AI-first software delivery](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-in/engineering/ai-first-software-delivery). We had a three-day workshop, discussed various techniques and ideas, and came up with our predictions for the next few years of Bahmni. We wanted to take a real wild swing at moving the AngularJS frontend to a more modern stack like React, built on FHIR (a standard for sharing health data). This was no laughing matter. But pumped up by the opportunities ahead, we wanted to take it on, because anything else would be easy.

The work kept growing. We worked on the [Research, Review, Rebuild](https://martinfowler.com/articles/research-review-rebuild.html) framework, which I presented on stage at the 2025 ThoughtWorks Xconf. Last year we partnered with an international agency to build their medical triage system on top of Bahmni. The engagement brought new challenges, customised workflows, and a range of improvements to Bahmni.

Along the way, we rebuilt and [released the clinical module](https://talk.openmrs.org/t/announcement-bahmni-lite-v2-0-release/49607) on a modern React UI, predominantly on FHIR. Most of our bets paid off, and we kept learning and sharing with the community as we could. This is a journey I am immensely proud of and would never trade for anything else.

## Epilogue

Like all great things, my journey with Bahmni comes to an end. For a long time now, I have been questioning my "worth" on the team. Many new folks have joined and made a huge impact on the project, and somewhere along the way I started feeling a little unsure of my place here, a little restless. I wanted to step out and "see the world," for I felt there were now enough hands on deck to do much more than I ever could alone. I still love being associated with the project in whatever ways I can, but I also want to work on new projects, try new tech stacks, meet new people, and grow my skillset. That feeling of being on borrowed time still haunts me, but I like to believe that I have made the most of the time I was given.
